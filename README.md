# Rightmove-vs-Autotrader-analysis
Financial and business performance analysis: Rightmove vs Auto Trader (2023-2025)
## Company Overview
Rightmove is a UK-based online property portal connecting home buyers, renters, and estate agents through property listings. It operates as a subscription-based marketplace, generating revenue primarily through fees charged to estate agents and developers for advertising properties on its platform, similarly to Autotrader which is also a UK-based online automotive marketplace who connects car buyers with dealerships and private sellers. Like Rightmove, it operates a subscription-based listing model, charging dealers for advertising vehicles rather than taking a cut of individual sales. Both companies share a similar business model: an asset-light, high-margin digital platform generating revenue from fees and subscriptions rather than physical inventory sales, making them a useful comparison despite operating in different markets.
## Key Financial Trends
- **Revenue growth:** Rightmove's revenue grew from £346.3m (2023) to £425.1m (2025), an increase of around 23% over the period. Auto Trader's revenue grew from £500.2m (2023) to £601.1m (2025), an increase of around 20%. Both companies had consistent year-on-year growth, with no declines across the 3-year period.
- **Profitability trend:** Both companies maintained high profitability during the 3-year period, though Rightmove's operating margin (68-75%) consistently outperformed Auto Trader's (55-63%) in every year measured.
- **Balance sheet strength:** Both companies had little to no debt. Rightmove held no borrowings across all 3 years, while Auto Trader reduced its debt from £57.5m in 2023 to £0m by 2025, fully repaying its outstanding borrowings (debt-to-equity falling from 0.11 to 0.00 over the same period).
## Key Observations
1. Margin gap is consistent, not incidental. Rightmove's operating margin exceeded Auto Trader's in every single year measured (for e.g. 68% vs 63% in 2025), suggesting a consistent difference in cost base or pricing power rather than a one-off result.
2. Both companies are moving towards, or maintaining a debt-free position. Auto Trader fully repaid its borrowings by 2025, while Rightmove carried no debt throughout. This is unusual for companies of this size and points to strong, self-reliant cash flow rather than reliance on external financing.
3. Rightmove's current ratio is declining, worth monitoring. Falling from 2.41 (2023) to 1.89 (2025), still comfortably above the 1.0 threshold, but the consistent downward trend is worth flagging as something to watch in future periods, even if not currently a concern.
 ## Competitor Comparison  
While both companies operate similar asset-light, subscription-based platform models, Rightmove is the more profitable of the two on every profitability measure examined. Its operating margin advantage has narrowed considerably over the period, from a 20-percentage point gap in 2023 (75% vs 55%) to just 5 points by 2025 (68% vs 63%), suggesting Auto Trader has been closing the profitability gap. Rightmove has also maintained a stronger current ratio throughout, indicating a more comfortable short-term financial position.
Auto Trader, meanwhile, generates a larger absolute revenue base (£601.1m vs Rightmove's £425.1m in 2025) and has shown the sharper improvement in its balance sheet, moving from a modest debt position to fully debt-free within the 3-year period, a shift not seen in Rightmove's more consistently debt-free position.
One point worth noting: Auto Trader's financial year runs to 31 March, while Rightmove's runs to 31 December, so the figures compared are not perfectly time-aligned, although both represent a full 12-month reporting period.
## Conclusion
Overall, both companies are financially strong, with consistent profit growth each year and minimal reliance on debt, Rightmove was debt-free throughout the period, while Auto Trader became fully debt-free by 2025. Despite Auto Trader's stronger balance sheet improvement and larger revenue base, Rightmove has been the more efficient and profitable of the two, with an operating margin advantage that, while narrowing from 20% in 2023 to 5% by 2025, held in Rightmove's favour throughout. One area worth investigating further is the split between core subscription revenue and any additional advertising or premium placement revenue, which could show whether margin strength comes from the core listing model or optional services.
